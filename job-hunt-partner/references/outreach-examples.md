# Outreach examples

These show the editing logic across different fields. The people and facts are invented. Adapt the structure to the user's real materials and never copy wording mechanically.

Each example is labelled with its move, so the sequence stays visible. Character counts include spaces.

---

## MOVE 1: connection notes

The note earns the accept. No question, no ask, no hook that begs a reply. Written to 200 characters.

### 1. Data analyst, healthcare

**Draft**

> Hi Priya, I just applied for the Senior Data Analyst role and I'm really passionate about your mission of making healthcare data accessible. I think my background makes me a perfect fit. Would love to pick your brain sometime!

`226 characters, over the limit`

**Rewrite**

> Hi Priya, I just applied for the Senior Data Analyst role. I spent three years building claims dashboards for a hospital network, so the cost-transparency work stood out. Would be great to connect.

`197 characters`

**Why it works**

- Replaces "passionate about your mission" and "perfect fit" with one fact that shows the fit.
- Drops "pick your brain", which asks for unpaid time from a stranger.
- Ties the proof to something specific the team does.

### 2. Backend engineer, fintech

**Draft**

> Hey Tom, saw the Backend Engineer opening on the payments team. I have 5 years of experience in Go, Kubernetes, PostgreSQL, Kafka and AWS and I've worked at two fintechs. Curious how the team is approaching scaling?

`215 characters`

**Rewrite**

> Hey Tom, I applied for the Backend Engineer role on payments. I rebuilt a ledger service in Go that runs our refunds, so reconciliation at your scale is a problem I know. Would be great to connect.

`197 characters`

**Why it works**

- A list of tools is a resume, not a reason. One piece of work replaces five keywords.
- Removes the closing question. Notes rarely get replies, so a question in one wastes the space.

### 3. Marketing manager, shared school

> Hi Ana, we both studied at Nova SBE. I just applied for the Marketing Manager role on your lifecycle team, where most of my last four years has been. Would be great to connect.

`176 characters`

**Why it works**

- The shared school does the work of earning the accept.
- One clause of relevance, no pitch.
- Sets up a rung 1 ask in move 2.

## MOVE 2: the DM after they accept

24 to 48 hours after the accept. Under 400 characters. The ask is named here.

### 4. Rung 0, routing ask, no relationship

> Hi Tom, thanks for connecting. I applied for the Backend Engineer role on Monday. Do you know who's leading hiring for it? Happy to go through the right channel rather than guess.

`179 characters`

**Why it works**

- Asks for a pointer, which costs him nothing and risks no reputation.
- The ask is in the second sentence.

### 5. Rung 1, referral ask, shared school

> Hi Ana, thanks for connecting. I applied for the Marketing Manager role today. I know it's outside your team, but would you be comfortable putting in a referral? Completely fine if not, and I can send everything you'd need in one message.

`238 characters`

**Why it works**

- The shared school earned the right to ask directly.
- "Would you be comfortable" plus "completely fine if not" gives a clean yes or no.
- Offering the packet up front makes the yes cheap.

### 6. Already applied, no referral basis

> Hi Priya, thanks for connecting. I applied for the Senior Data Analyst role last week. The claims dashboards I mentioned cut monthly reporting from five days to one. If you think it's worth a look, I'd appreciate you flagging it to the team. Totally fine if not.

`262 characters`

**Why it works**

- Adds one new proof point with a result, instead of repeating the note.
- Names the action (flag it), the recipient (the team) and gives an exit.
- This is the honest replacement for "Is there anything worth highlighting?", which makes the reader guess what help is wanted.

## MOVE 4: follow-up on silence

7 to 10 days after move 2. Two sentences. No new ask.

### 7. No reply

> Hi Tom, following up on the below in case it got buried. Either way, good luck with the search, it looks like a strong team.

`124 characters`

**Why it works**

- Does not repeat the ask, which would read as pressure.
- Ends warm, so the connection survives the silence.

## CONVERSATION INVITES

No live posting, or the posting is secondary.

### 8. Career changer asking a specific question

**Draft**

> Hi Dr. Okafor, I really admire your work and would love to pick your brain about moving into UX research. Let me know if you're free for a coffee chat!

**Rewrite**

> Hi Dr. Okafor, I'm a clinical psychologist moving into UX research. Your talk on running usability studies with patients in mind was the first time I saw my two fields connect. How did you justify research time to product teams early on? Worth 15 minutes, or happy to ask it here if that's easier.

`297 characters`

**Why it works**

- Names one thing she said, not her company or her "journey".
- One narrow question she can answer without preparing.
- Two options. The message option costs her nothing.
- A career changer can ask for advice, as long as the question is specific.

### 9. Give first, no ask

> Hi Jonas, we haven't met. You posted about your team switching to dbt and losing a week to testing. We went through the same migration last spring and I wrote up the test setup that finally worked. Happy to share it if it's useful. No ask on my side.

`250 characters`

**Why it works**

- Leads with something useful the sender already has.
- "No ask on my side" is true, and it is what makes a later message land.

### 10. After a rejection, keeping it warm

> Hi Marta, they went another way on the Product Designer role, which is fair. I'd still like to stay in touch, the onboarding problem you described is one I keep running into. If anything opens up on that side later, I'd be glad to hear about it.

`245 characters`

**Why it works**

- No bitterness and no arguing with the decision.
- Keeps one specific thread alive instead of a vague "let's stay in touch".

## THE PACKET

Sent within the hour of a yes.

### 11. After someone agrees to refer

> Thanks Ana, that means a lot. Everything in one place:
>
> Role: Marketing Manager, Lifecycle
> Link: [direct job URL]
> Req ID: [if there is one]
> Applied under: [the email on the application]
>
> One line you can paste:
> "Lifecycle marketer, six years, rebuilt the onboarding email program at a subscription app and lifted 30-day retention from 41% to 48%."
>
> Resume attached. Happy to answer anything if they come back to you.

**Why it works**

- She does not have to open a second tab or ask a single question.
- The paste line comes from the resume and ends on a fact.
